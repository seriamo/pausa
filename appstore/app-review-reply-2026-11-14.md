1. What the app does and the problem it solves

Pausa helps people rest their eyes while they work at a Mac. Long stretches of close focus on a screen lower the blink rate and cause eye strain. Eye care professionals recommend the 20-20-20 rule (every 20 minutes, look 20 feet away for 20 seconds), but people forget it once they are concentrating.

Pausa lives in the menu bar. Every few minutes (20 by default, adjustable from 5 to 60), it lights the edge of the screen for a few seconds (3 to 20): a bar across the menu bar, a bar with a soft glow, or a glow around the whole border, in the color and intensity the user picks. The light is a click-through overlay. It never takes focus, never blocks input, makes no sound, needs no dismissal and fades on its own, so the user notices it in peripheral vision, looks away and carries on. Pausa skips reminders while a fullscreen app is in front, waits while the Mac is locked, can be paused, and can start at login.

2. Intended user

People who spend long hours at a Mac, such as developers, designers, writers and students, who focus for long stretches, are often in calls or presenting, and want a reminder that doesn't break their concentration. Also people with eye strain who have been told to follow the 20-20-20 rule.

3. The gap it addresses

Most break reminders interrupt you to make you rest: a full-screen break window, a notification or a sound that you have to dismiss or postpone. People who are focused or presenting switch them off and get no reminders at all. Pausa's cue is peripheral and non-modal, so it is safe to leave running all day, and it steps aside for fullscreen presentations. It has no account, subscription, ads or in-app purchases.
